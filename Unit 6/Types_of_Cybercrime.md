# CSE332: INDUSTRY ETHICS AND LEGAL ISSUES
# UNIT VI — CYBERCRIME & THE IT ACT SECTIONS (INDIA)
### Quick Reference Sheet: Types of Cybercrime & Legal Provisions

---

> **📌 Reference Context:** This sheet outlines the major categories of cybercrime, how they operate, and their corresponding sections under the Indian Information Technology (IT) Act, 2000 (including amendments).

---

## 📊 Types of Cybercrime — Full List

| Crime | What happens | IT Act Section |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Hacking** | Unauthorized access to a computer system | Sec 66 |
| **Identity Theft** | Using someone's digital identity fraudulently | Sec 66C |
| **Phishing** | Fake emails/sites to steal credentials | Sec 66D |
| **Cyber Terrorism** | Attacks on national security/infrastructure | Sec 66F |
| **Obscene Content** | Publishing obscene/sexual content online | Sec 67 |
| **Ransomware** | Encrypts data, demands payment to unlock | Sec 66, 43 |
| **DoS / DDoS** | Flooding server to make it unavailable | Sec 43, 66 |
| **Cyberstalking** | Harassing someone repeatedly online | Sec 66A *(Struck down)* |
| **Data Theft** | Stealing confidential data from systems | Sec 43, 66 |
| **Vishing** | Voice phishing — fraud via phone call | Sec 66D |

---

## 🔍 Detailed Analysis of IT Act Sections

### 1. Section 43: Penalty and Compensation for Damage to Computer System
* **Offenses Covered:** Data theft, ransomware, DoS/DDoS attacks, introducing viruses, damage to computer networks, copying/extracting data without permission.
* **Provision:** Civil liability; the offender is liable to pay damages by way of compensation to the person affected.

### 2. Section 66: Computer Related Offenses
* **Offenses Covered:** Hacking, ransomware, DDoS/DoS, and general computer damage done dishonestly or fraudulently.
* **Punishment:** Imprisonment up to 3 years, or a fine up to ₹5 lakh, or both.

### 3. Section 66A: Punishment for Sending Offensive Messages *(STRUCK DOWN)*
* **Offenses Covered:** Sending offensive, menacing, or annoying messages via communication service.
* **Important Note:** Struck down entirely by the Supreme Court of India in **2015** in the landmark case ***Shreya Singhal v. Union of India*** because it was deemed overly broad and violated the constitutional right to freedom of speech and expression (Article 19(1)(a)).

### 4. Section 66C: Punishment for Identity Theft
* **Offenses Covered:** Fraudulently or dishonestly making use of another person's digital signature, password, or unique identification feature (e.g., biometrics).
* **Punishment:** Imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine up to ₹1 lakh.

### 5. Section 66D: Punishment for Cheating by Personation using Computer Resource
* **Offenses Covered:** Phishing (impersonation via fake websites/emails), vishing (voice impersonation over phone calls), online financial scams where the perpetrator impersonates a legitimate organization or individual.
* **Punishment:** Imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine up to ₹1 lakh.

### 6. Section 66F: Punishment for Cyber Terrorism
* **Offenses Covered:** Denying access to authorized persons, unauthorized access to critical infrastructure, or attempting to penetrate security systems of national significance to threaten unity, integrity, security, or sovereignty of India.
* **Punishment:** Imprisonment which may extend to **life imprisonment**.

### 7. Section 67: Punishment for Publishing or Transmitting Obscene Material in Electronic Form
* **Offenses Covered:** Publishing or transmitting materials that are lascivious, appeal to prurient interest, or deprave/corrupt minds.
* **Punishment:** 
  * *First conviction:* Imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine up to ₹5 lakh.
  * *Subsequent conviction:* Imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to ₹10 lakh.

## 💡 Quick Exam Revision Tips

1. **Dual Liability (Sec 43 & 66):** Cybercrimes like Ransomware, DDoS, and Data Theft are prosecuted under both **Section 43** (for civil damages/compensation) and **Section 66** (for criminal prosecution and imprisonment).
2. **The 66A Exception:** If a question asks about cyberstalking or offensive messages under Section 66A, always remember that **Section 66A is no longer legally enforceable** as it was struck down in 2015. Such offenses are now prosecuted under relevant IPC sections (like Section 354D for cyberstalking).
3. **Phishing vs. Vishing (Sec 66D):** Both phishing (text/web-based) and vishing (voice call-based) involve impersonation to cheat, bringing them both under **Section 66D**.

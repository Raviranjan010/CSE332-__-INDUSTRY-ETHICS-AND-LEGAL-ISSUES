## 🔷 SECTION A: Highest Probability MCQs (Core Syllabus & Exam Classics)

**Q1. What is the fundamental difference between LAW and ETHICS in the context of information security?**
- A) Ethics are legally binding on all citizens in a country.
- B) **Law carries the sanction and enforcement power of a governing authority.** ✅
- C) Laws are always morally correct, while ethics may not be.
- D) Ethics are written and published by government bodies.

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Laws are formal rules adopted by a governing authority and enforced with legal sanctions (fines, jail). Ethics are moral or professional guidelines followed voluntarily. Breaking ethics does not result in state-enforced punishment.

---

**Q2. What is the key difference between an ORGANIZATIONAL POLICY and a LAW?**
- A) **Policies are internal organizational guidelines; laws are government-mandated and apply across jurisdictions.** ✅
- B) Laws apply only to private companies; policies apply to governments.
- C) Policies carry criminal penalties; laws carry only civil penalties.
- D) Both policies and laws are enforced by courts.

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Policies (e.g., AUP, Password Policy) are internal documents created by an organization to govern employee behavior. Laws (e.g., GDPR, IT Act 2000) are enacted by a government and enforceable in court.

---

**Q3. What is ORGANIZATIONAL LIABILITY in cybersecurity?**
- A) The legal obligation of employees to report all cyberattacks to the police.
- B) **An organization can be held legally and financially responsible if it fails to exercise due care in protecting data.** ✅
- C) The duty of software vendors to patch their products within 24 hours.
- D) A company's responsibility to train all employees in ethical hacking.

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Organizational liability means a company can face lawsuits, regulatory fines, and compensation claims from affected parties if its negligence (failure to implement reasonable security/due care) leads to a data breach.

- B) **Acceptable Use Policy (AUP).** ✅
- C) Service Level Agreement (SLA)
- D) Indian Penal Code (IPC)

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** An AUP is an internal corporate policy document that defines what is acceptable and unacceptable use of company IT resources. Violation is handled through HR, not courts.

**Q5. Which Indian law is the PRIMARY legislation governing cybercrime and digital activities?**
- A) Indian Penal Code, 1860
- B) Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
- C) **Information Technology (IT) Act, 2000.** ✅
- D) Indian Patents Act, 1970

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The IT Act, 2000 is India's foundational cyber law. It covers hacking (Section 66), identity theft (Section 66C), cyber fraud (Section 66D), and provides legal recognition to electronic records.

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The DPDP Act 2023 is India's dedicated personal data protection law. It mandates explicit user consent, gives data principals rights to erase/correct their data, and sets penalties up to ₹250 crore.

**Q7. Section 66A of the IT Act, 2000 was:**
- A) Enhanced and strengthened in 2023.
- B) **Struck down by the Supreme Court of India.** ✅
- C) Replaced directly by the DPDP Act.
- D) Used to penalize software piracy.

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Section 66A was completely struck down by the Supreme Court in the landmark case *Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015)* because it was unconstitutionally vague and violated Article 19(1)(a) (freedom of speech).

**Explanation:** Data is distributed outside the physical office perimeter across multiple clouds, requiring cloud-native visibility and protection tools.

**Q67. Arrange the following from FIRST to LAST in a typical cyberattack defense lifecycle: (1) Patch the vulnerability, (2) Detect the intrusion, (3) Contain and isolate the affected system, (4) Conduct post-incident review.**
- A) 1 → 2 → 3 → 4
- B) **2 → 3 → 1 → 4** ✅
- C) 3 → 2 → 1 → 4
- D) 4 → 1 → 2 → 3

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The correct order of incident response is: detection (2), immediate containment/isolation (3), system eradication/patching (1), followed by post-incident review (4).

**Q68. Which of the following scenarios represents a supply chain attack?**
- A) An attacker phishes a company's CEO.
- B) Ransomware encrypts a company's files directly.
- C) **An attacker compromises a third-party software update to infect all customers of that software.** ✅
- D) An insider exfiltrates data to a USB drive.

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Supply chain attacks compromise a vendor (e.g., software update) to infect the target organization (e.g., SolarWinds breach).

---

**Q69. True or False: Firewalls alone are sufficient to protect an organization from all modern cyber threats.**
- A) True
- B) **False** ✅

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Firewalls only protect the perimeter. Phishing, social engineering, compromised credentials, and insider threats easily bypass firewalls.

---

**Q70. True or False: Anti-virus software can reliably detect and stop zero-day malware.**
- A) True
- B) **False** ✅

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Traditional anti-virus depends on known signatures. Zero-day malware has no signature, rendering traditional AV ineffective without behavioral EDR tools.

---

**Q71. True or False: The Colonial Pipeline attack was a zero-day vulnerability exploit.**
- A) True
- B) **False** ✅

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The Colonial Pipeline breach resulted from credential leakage/compromise on a legacy VPN account that lacked MFA, not a zero-day exploit.

---

**Q72. True or False: Cybersecurity is a one-time project that ends once all tools are installed.**
- A) True
- B) **False** ✅

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Cybersecurity is a continuous lifecycle of risk management, requiring constant monitoring, patch updates, training, and policy adaptations.

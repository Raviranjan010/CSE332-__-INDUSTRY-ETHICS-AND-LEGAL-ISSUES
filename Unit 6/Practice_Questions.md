## 📌 PART A — SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS (2-3 Marks)

**Q1. Distinguish between law and ethics in the context of information security.**
*Answer:* Laws are formal rules adopted and enforced by a governing authority with clear legal sanctions (fines, jail). Ethics are relatively fixed moral principles/customs of a group (like professional codes) that guide conduct without state-enforced sanctions. The key difference is that law carries government-enforced penalties, whereas ethics carry internal or social consequences.

**Q2. Differentiate between policy and law in information security. Give examples.**
*Answer:* Policies are internal guidelines set by an organization to govern employee behavior (e.g., Acceptable Use Policy, Password Policy). Laws are legally enforceable mandates set by governments that apply to everyone in a jurisdiction (e.g., GDPR, IT Act 2000).

**Q3. What is organizational liability? How does legal counsel help?**
*Answer:* Organizational liability means an entity can be held legally and financially responsible for data breaches and non-compliance if it fails to exercise due care. Legal counsel helps organizations navigate global cybersecurity regulations, handle breach notifications, draft compliance policies, and manage litigation risk.

**Q4. List any four tools used in penetration testing and web application security.**
*Answer:*
1. **Nmap:** Network mapping and port scanning.
2. **Metasploit:** Framework for testing and executing exploits.
3. **Burp Suite:** Web application security auditing.
4. **Kali Linux:** Penetration testing operating system pre-packaged with security tools.

**Q5. Explain the difference between arbitration and mediation in ADR.**
*Answer:* In mediation, a neutral third party (mediator) helps the disputing parties reach a voluntary, mutually acceptable agreement but has no power to impose a decision. In arbitration, the neutral third party (arbitrator) acts as a private judge, conducts hearings, and makes a legally binding decision (award) that both parties must follow.

## 📌 PART B — LONG ANSWER QUESTIONS (5-7 Marks)

**Q6. What is cybercrime? Classify the different types of cybercrimes and explain their rising trends.**
*Answer:*
Cybercrime refers to any illegal activity involving computers, networks, or digital devices.
* **Classification:**
  1. **Cyber Fraud:** Deceiving individuals online for financial gain (phishing, identity theft).
  2. **Hacking & Data Breaches:** Unauthorized access to steal, modify, or delete sensitive data.
  3. **Intellectual Property Theft:** Unauthorized copy/use of copyrighted software or patents.
  4. **Cyber Terrorism:** Attacking critical national infrastructures (telecom, power grids).
* **Rising Trends:** Driven by increased digital dependence (digital banking, IoT), lack of security awareness, and high financial motivation (ransomware paid via cryptocurrency). A cyberattack is estimated to occur every 39 seconds globally.

**Q7. Explain the key cyber laws in India: the IT Act 2000, IPC sections related to cybercrime, and the DPDP Act 2023.**
*Answer:*
1. **Information Technology (IT) Act, 2000:** India's primary cyber law. Penalizes hacking (Section 66), identity theft (Section 66C), and online fraud (Section 66D). Section 66A was struck down in 2015.
2. **Indian Penal Code (IPC):** Sections 419/420 are used for cheating/phishing scams, Section 500 for cyber defamation, and Section 354D for cyberstalking.
3. **Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023:** Regulates personal data processing. Mandates explicit consent, gives data principals rights to erase/correct data, and sets penalties up to ₹250 crore for breaches.

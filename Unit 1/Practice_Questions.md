## 📌 PART A — SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS (2-3 Marks)

**Q1. Define ethics. How is it different from law?**
*Answer:* Ethics refers to the moral principles or values that guide a person's behavior in deciding what is right and wrong. The key difference is that law carries the sanction of a governing authority (state/government) and is legally enforceable, whereas ethics do not have formal government enforcement but are guided by personal and social mores.

**Q2. What is meant by integrity in the context of professional behavior?**
*Answer:* Integrity means doing the right thing even when no one is watching. It refers to the quality of being honest and consistently adhering to strong moral principles in all professional actions.

**Q3. List any four ethical principles that guide business behavior.**
*Answer:*
1. Honesty (truthfulness in all dealings)
2. Fairness (non-discriminatory treatment of stakeholders)
3. Responsibility (accountability for business impacts)
4. Integrity (consistency with moral principles)

**Q4. What is a Code of Ethics? What are its key components?**
*Answer:* A Code of Ethics is a formal document outlining the ethical rules and standards that guide behavior within an organization. Key components include: mission and values statement, standards for professional behavior, conflict of interest policies, confidentiality guidelines, and reporting mechanisms.

**Q5. Explain the PAPA framework in IT ethics.**
*Answer:* The PAPA framework was developed by Richard Mason to highlight the key ethical issues of the information age:
1. **Privacy:** What information about a person can be collected and stored?
2. **Accuracy:** Who is responsible for the authenticity and precision of data?
3. **Property:** Who owns the information and intellectual property?
4. **Accessibility:** Who has the right to access the information?

**Q6. What is the role of a whistle-blower in maintaining corporate ethics?**
*Answer:* A whistle-blower reports unethical, illegal, or unsafe practices within an organization to internal management or external authorities. They help expose wrongdoing, prevent harm, and maintain transparency and accountability in corporate culture.

**Q7. List any four common ethical issues faced by IT users.**
*Answer:*
1. Software piracy (using unlicensed software)
2. Sharing confidential personal data without consent
3. Accessing inappropriate content on company networks
4. Plagiarism and academic dishonesty

**Q8. What is software piracy? Mention its different types.**
*Answer:* Software piracy is using or distributing software without a valid license. Types include:
1. End-user piracy (installing on extra machines)
2. Internet piracy (downloading illegal copies)
3. Hard-disk loading (pre-installing pirated software on sold machines)
4. Counterfeiting (selling fake copies)

**Q9. Define an Acceptable Use Policy (AUP). What does it typically contain?**
*Answer:* An AUP is an internal organizational policy defining permitted and prohibited uses of company IT resources. It typically contains guidelines on data handling, internet usage, password security, prohibited sites, and consequences for violations.

**Q10. What is the ACM Code of Ethics? List its key principles.**
*Answer:* The ACM (Association for Computing Machinery) Code of Ethics is a professional code guiding computing professionals. Key principles include: contributing to society and human well-being, avoiding harm, being honest and trustworthy, being fair and non-discriminatory, and respecting privacy and confidentiality.

**Q11. Mention any three government initiatives in India for women empowerment in technology.**
*Answer:*
1. NASSCOM Women in Tech (training programs)
2. Beti Bachao Beti Padhao (promoting girl child education)
3. Digital Sakhi (digital literacy for rural women)

**Q12. What are "moral rights" in the context of copyright?**
*Answer:* Moral rights are non-economic rights of an author. They include the right to claim authorship of the work (paternity right) and the right to oppose any distortion or mutilation of the work that harms their reputation (integrity right).

**Q13. State two reasons why IT ethics is unique compared to traditional ethics.**
*Answer:*
1. Digital information can be copied infinitely at zero cost.
2. Technology evolves faster than the laws can keep pace, leaving ethical gray areas.

**Q14. What is cyberbullying? Give two examples.**
*Answer:* Cyberbullying is using digital platforms to harass, abuse, or threaten someone. Examples: sending abusive messages on social media, or sharing private photos of someone without their consent.

**Q15. What is the difference between ethics and integrity?**
*Answer:* Ethics refers to external guidelines or rules of conduct provided by society or profession. Integrity refers to an individual's internal consistency of character and their personal choice to follow those ethical rules.

## 📌 PART B — LONG ANSWER QUESTIONS (5-7 Marks)

**Q16. Explain the different types of ethics (normative, descriptive, meta, applied) with examples from the IT industry.**
*Answer:*
1. **Normative Ethics:** Focuses on what people *ought* to do (rules and duties). Example: ACM Code of Ethics instructing IT professionals to respect privacy.
2. **Descriptive Ethics:** Studies what people *actually* believe is right or wrong. Example: Surveying IT users on whether they think downloading pirated software is acceptable.
3. **Meta-Ethics:** Focuses on the meaning of ethical concepts. Example: What do we mean by "privacy" or "property" in the digital age?
4. **Applied Ethics:** How ethical principles are applied to real-world situations. Example: Business ethics, engineering ethics, or IT ethics (analyzing the Cambridge Analytica data privacy scandal).

**Q17. Discuss the concept of "ethical decision making" in detail. Explain the step-by-step ethical decision-making model.**
*Answer:* Ethical decision making is the process of evaluating choices based on moral principles rather than personal gain. The step-by-step model:
1. **Recognize the ethical issue:** Identify that a moral dilemma exists.
2. **Gather relevant facts:** Collect all details and clear up assumptions.
3. **Identify stakeholders:** Who is affected by this decision?
4. **Consider ethical principles:** Evaluate options under Utilitarianism, Deontology, etc.
5. **Evaluate available options:** What choices do we have?
6. **Make the decision:** Choose the most ethical path.
7. **Act and reflect:** Implement the decision and assess the outcome.

**Q18. Compare and contrast Utilitarianism, Deontology, Virtue Ethics, and Justice theories as frameworks for ethical decision making. Give IT examples for each.**
*Answer:*
* **Utilitarianism:** Focuses on outcomes; chooses the action that maximizes the greatest good for the greatest number. Example: Releasing buggy software to meet a critical deadline that saves a company from bankruptcy.
* **Deontology:** Duty-based ethics; actions are right or wrong based on rules, regardless of consequences. Example: Refusing to lie to customers about a software defect, even if it hurts short-term sales.
* **Virtue Ethics:** Focuses on character; asks what a virtuous person would do. Example: A whistle-blower exposing security flaws out of honesty and public responsibility.
* **Justice/Fairness:** Focuses on equal and fair distribution of benefits and burdens. Example: Equal pay for equal coding work regardless of gender.

**Q19. What is meant by "creating an ethical work environment"? Discuss the components and strategies with examples from IT companies.**
*Answer:* An ethical work environment is one where organizational systems, leaders, and policies encourage employees to behave ethically. Components:
1. **Leadership (Tone at the Top):** Leaders modeling ethical behavior (e.g., Satya Nadella at Microsoft).
2. **Code of Conduct:** Clear policies on conflict of interest, harassment, and security.
3. **Open Communication:** Psychological safety where employees can report issues without fear (ethics hotlines).
4. **Recognition:** Rewarding ethical choices and enforcing zero-tolerance for misconduct.

**Q20. Explain the PAPA framework. Discuss how each element (Privacy, Accuracy, Property, Accessibility) applies to modern social media platforms like Facebook or Instagram.**
*Answer:*
* **Privacy:** What user profile and browsing data is harvested and shared with advertisers? (Facebook-Cambridge Analytica issue).
* **Accuracy:** Who ensures that user profiles, news shared, or advertisements are factual and free of fake news?
* **Property:** Who owns the photos and content uploaded? (Users retain ownership, but grant platforms broad licenses).
* **Accessibility:** Who has access to the platform? Is it accessible to people with visual/auditory impairments?

## 📌 PART C — CASE STUDY QUESTIONS (10 Marks)

**Q21. Case Study: Employee Email Monitoring**
*Situation: Riya is an IT professional asked by her manager to create a system to monitor employees' personal emails on office networks without their knowledge, supposedly to prevent data leaks.*
* **What is the ethical dilemma?** Riya faces a conflict between employee privacy rights (ethical) and employer loyalty / manager orders (professional duty).
* **Which principles apply?** The ACM Code of Ethics (respect privacy, avoid harm) and IEEE Code of Ethics (highest standard of professional conduct).
* **What should Riya do?** Riya should express her concerns to her manager, pointing out the lack of transparency. She should suggest implementing a transparent Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) where employees are notified that company networks are monitored, rather than conducting surveillance in secret.

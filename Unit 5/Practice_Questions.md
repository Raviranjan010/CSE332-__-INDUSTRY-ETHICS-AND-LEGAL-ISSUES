## 📌 PART A — SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS (2-3 Marks)

**Q1. Define IT and ITES. What is the main difference between them?**
*Answer:* Information Technology (IT) refers to the software, hardware, infrastructure, and systems used to process, store, and secure electronic data. IT-Enabled Services (ITES) are the actual business processes (like BPO, customer support, data entry, legal outsourcing) that are executed remotely using information technology. The difference is that IT is the technology itself, whereas ITES represents the services delivered using that technology.

**Q2. What is the difference between a product-based IT company and a service-based IT company?**
*Answer:* A product-based IT company designs and sells its own proprietary software or hardware (e.g., Microsoft Office, iPhones). A service-based IT company provides technology consulting, custom development, and system integration services to other companies (e.g., TCS, Infosys).

**Q3. What is NASSCOM? What was its launch year and its primary role?**
*Answer:* NASSCOM (National Association of Software and Services Companies) was established in 1988 as the premier trade association for the IT-BPM industry in India. Its role is policy advocacy, skill development (such as FutureSkills Prime), trade promotion, and supporting start-ups.

**Q4. What is STPI? How does it help Indian IT exports?**
*Answer:* Software Technology Parks of India (STPI) was established in 1991 under MeitY to promote computer software exports. It helps IT exports by providing dedicated high-speed data communication links, incubation infrastructure, single-window clearances, and historical tax exemptions under Section 10A.

**Q5. Give two examples of hybrid tech business models and how they operate.**
*Answer:*
1. **Microsoft:** Sells products (Windows licenses, Surface devices) and also provides services (Azure cloud, Microsoft 365 SaaS subscriptions).
2. **Amazon:** Sells physical retail products and devices (Echo, Kindle) and also offers massive cloud infrastructure services (AWS).

## 📌 PART B — LONG ANSWER QUESTIONS (5-7 Marks)

**Q6. Compare product-based and service-based IT companies across key parameters: scalability, risk, profit margins, and capital required.**
*Answer:*
* **Scalability:** Product-based companies have extremely high scalability because software can be copied and sold infinitely at zero marginal cost. Service-based companies scale linearly because increasing client load requires hiring more billable employees.
* **Risk:** Product-based companies carry high initial risk because they must fund R&D before finding out if the product will succeed in the market. Service-based companies have low risk since projects are pre-funded and commissioned by clients.
* **Profit Margins:** Product-based companies enjoy high profit margins once R&D costs are recovered. Service-based companies have lower, tighter margins due to high employee salary overheads.
* **Capital Required:** Product-based companies require high initial capital for R&D. Service-based companies can launch with low capital by focusing on hiring and office setup.

**Q7. Discuss the business case for diversity in the IT workforce. Why does gender and ethnic diversity matter?**
*Answer:*
Diversity in the IT workforce refers to having representation from different genders, races, ethnicities, and backgrounds.
1. **Avoiding Product Bias:** A homogeneous team might build products with blind spots (e.g., facial recognition algorithms trained only on light skin tones because the development team lacked diversity).
2. **Expanded Talent Pool:** Tapping into underrepresented demographics helps solve the acute tech talent shortage.
3. **Enhanced Innovation:** Diverse teams bring multiple perspectives to problem-solving, resulting in better design decisions, more creative solutions, and higher profitability.
4. **Market Alignment:** Tech products serve a global, diverse consumer base; having a diverse workforce ensures products align with global customer needs.

## 📌 PART C — CASE STUDY QUESTIONS (10 Marks)

**Q8. Case Study: Aadhaar Card Project**
*Situation: The Government of India launched the Aadhaar project, collecting biometrics (iris and fingerprint scans) and issuing a unique 12-digit number to over 1.3 billion citizens.*
* **Analyze the global impact of this IT project:** Aadhaar serves as the foundational digital identity. It enabled the Unified Payments Interface (UPI) and direct benefit transfer (DBT), preventing leaks in welfare distribution and driving massive financial inclusion.
* **Discuss the IT ethics concerns raised by this project:**
  1. *Privacy:* Storing sensitive biometrics of 1.3 billion citizens raises data privacy risks.
  2. *Security:* Incidents of unauthorized data access (data leaks) highlighted vulnerability issues.
  3. *Accuracy:* Incorrect biometric scans or names could exclude vulnerable citizens from accessing food rations or banking services.

## 📌 PART A — SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS (2-3 Marks)

**Q1. Define a startup as per DPIIT. How is it different from a small business?**
*Answer:* As per DPIIT (Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade), an entity is considered a Startup if:
1. It is incorporated as a Private Limited Company, LLP, or Partnership firm.
2. Its age is up to 10 years from incorporation.
3. Its annual turnover does not exceed ₹100 crore in any financial year.
4. It works towards innovation, development, or improvement of products or services with a scalable business model.
It differs from a small business because a startup focuses on high growth and high scalability using technology, while a small business operates within local constraints with stable growth.

**Q2. What are the key benefits of DPIIT recognition under Startup India?**
*Answer:* Key benefits include:
1. 3-year income tax holiday (tax exemption).
2. 80% rebate on patent filing and 50% rebate on trademark filing fees.
3. Self-certification under 9 labour and 3 environmental laws.
4. Access to Fund of Funds (₹10,000 Cr) and fast-track patent examination.

**Q3. What is the Fund of Funds (FFS) under Startup India? Who manages it?**
*Answer:* The Fund of Funds for Startups (FFS) is a ₹10,000 crore fund set up by the government to invest in SEBI-registered Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs) which in turn invest in startups. It is managed by SIDBI (Small Industries Development Bank of India).

**Q4. Explain MUDRA loans and their three categories.**
*Answer:* Micro Units Development & Refinance Agency (MUDRA) provides collateral-free loans to micro-businesses. The three categories:
1. **Shishu:** Loans up to ₹50,000 (for new/early businesses).
2. **Kishor:** Loans from ₹50,000 to ₹5 lakh (for expanding businesses).
3. **Tarun:** Loans from ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh (for established micro-enterprises).

**Q5. What is the Stand-Up India scheme? Who is it for?**
*Answer:* Stand-Up India is a scheme launched to promote entrepreneurship at the grassroots level. It provides bank loans between ₹10 lakh and ₹1 crore to at least one Scheduled Caste (SC) or Scheduled Tribe (ST) borrower and at least one Woman borrower per bank branch for setting up greenfield enterprises.

**Q6. What is the role of NASSCOM in India's startup ecosystem?**
*Answer:* NASSCOM acts as a trade body and advocacy group. Its *10,000 Startups* initiative (launched in 2013) supports startups through incubation, mentoring, funding access, and industry connect events to foster digital innovation.

**Q7. Differentiate between an incubator and an accelerator.**
*Answer:* An incubator helps early-stage startups refine their ideas, build MVPs, and establish business plans over a long duration (1–2 years), providing office space and mentorship. An accelerator helps existing startups with an MVP and customer traction scale up rapidly through intensive, short-term cohorts (3–6 months) and seed funding.

**Q8. What is STPI? List its key benefits for IT companies.**
*Answer:* Software Technology Parks of India (STPI) is an autonomous government society set up in 1991 to promote software exports. Key benefits:
1. 100% FDI allowed.
2. Duty-free import of hardware/software.
3. Tax benefits on software export profits.
4. Incubation infrastructure and high-speed data communication links.

**Q9. What is an SEZ? How does it differ from STPI?**
*Answer:* A Special Economic Zone (SEZ) is a specifically demarcated duty-free enclave deemed to be foreign territory for trade operations and tariff duties. It differs from STPI because: SEZs are multi-sectoral (manufacturing, services, textile), require a minimum land size, and have a 15-year sliding scale tax benefit model, while STPI is specifically for IT/software exports without land size limits.

**Q10. What is the SBIR program in the USA? Explain its three phases.**
*Answer:* Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR) is a highly competitive US government program that awards research grants to small businesses to perform R&D with commercial potential.
* **Phase I:** Feasibility and proof of concept ($50,000–$275,000; 6 months).
* **Phase II:** R&D and prototype development (up to $2 million; 2 years).
* **Phase III:** Commercialization (no direct SBIR funding, private funding needed).

**Q11. What is the STTR program? How does it differ from SBIR?**
*Answer:* Small Business Technology Transfer (STTR) is another US grant program. The key difference is that STTR requires the small business to formally collaborate with a non-profit research institution (e.g., a university) where the research institution must perform at least 30% of the work.

**Q12. What is the NSF? What kind of grants does it offer?**
*Answer:* The National Science Foundation (NSF) is a US government agency that funds scientific research. It offers non-equity grants to support early-stage technological innovations through the SBIR/STTR programs.

**Q13. What is CGTMSE? How does it help startups?**
*Answer:* Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE) is a trust set up by the government and SIDBI to provide credit guarantees to financial institutions. It helps startups obtain collateral-free working capital or term loans up to ₹2 crore.

**Q14. Mention the key laws governing startups in India.**
*Answer:*
1. The Companies Act, 2013 (governing incorporation and corporate rules).
2. The LLP Act, 2008 (governing Limited Liability Partnerships).
3. The Income Tax Act, 1961 (governing tax exemptions e.g. Section 80-IAC).
4. The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016 (governing winding up).

**Q15. What is the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) 2016? How does it help startups?**
*Answer:* The IBC, 2016 is a law that consolidates the legal framework for resolving insolvency of individuals and corporates. Under the "Fast Track Insolvency Resolution" provision, recognized startups with simple debt structures can wind up and exit their businesses within 90 days (compared to the standard 180 days).

## 📌 PART B — LONG ANSWER QUESTIONS (5-7 Marks)

**Q16. Describe the Startup India initiative in detail — its objectives, benefits, and impact on India's startup ecosystem.**
*Answer:*
Launched on January 16, 2016, by the Government of India, the initiative aims to build a strong ecosystem that is conducive to the growth of startups.
* **Objectives:** Promote entrepreneurship, drive economic growth, and generate large-scale employment.
* **Key Pillars:**
  1. **Simplification & Handholding:** Mobile app for registration, fast-track patent examination, and easy winding up.
  2. **Funding & Incentives:** Tax holidays, capital gains exemptions, and the ₹10,000 Cr Fund of Funds.
  3. **Industry-Academia Partnership:** Setting up incubators, organizing startup fests, and promoting research parks.
* **Impact:** Grown India's startup base from a few hundreds in 2016 to over 100,000+ DPIIT-recognized startups by 2023, making India the third-largest startup ecosystem in the world.

**Q17. Compare STPI and SEZ schemes for IT companies. In which situations would a startup choose STPI over SEZ?**
*Answer:*
* **STPI:** Software Technology Parks of India. It has no physical boundaries. IT firms can register anywhere. It is dedicated solely to IT/software exports. Best suited for small IT startups, as they can start in a rented office anywhere without huge capital.
* **SEZ:** Special Economic Zone. It requires a physically fenced area, custom clearances at the gate, and has a minimum land requirement. It covers multiple sectors (services, manufacturing).
* **Startup Choice:** A startup would choose STPI over SEZ if they want a smaller, flexible setup with lower infrastructure costs and do not need physical enclaves. They would choose SEZ if they are a larger enterprise needing duty-free imports of heavy physical machinery or massive office parks.

## 📌 PART C — CASE STUDY QUESTIONS (10 Marks)

**Q18. Case Study: Funding a Tech Startup**
*Situation: A 25-year-old engineer from a small town wants to start an IT company providing cybersecurity services to SMBs. He has limited capital.*
* **What government schemes can he access?**
  1. **DPIIT Startup Recognition:** Secure tax exemptions and fast-track patents.
  2. **MUDRA (Tarun Category) or CGTMSE:** Secure collateral-free working capital loan up to ₹10 lakh (MUDRA) or up to ₹2 crore (CGTMSE).
  3. **Stand-Up India:** If the founder is an SC/ST or a woman, they can access loans between ₹10 lakh and ₹1 crore.
* **What company structure should he choose?**
  * He should choose a **Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)** or a **One Person Company (OPC)**. An LLP provides limited liability and has lower compliance costs than a Private Limited company, making it ideal for a solo founder with limited capital.
* **Outline Business Plan:**
  1. *Executive Summary:* Provide cybersecurity audits to local SMBs.
  2. *Market Analysis:* Target local retail and clinics needing data security (compliant with DPDP Act).
  3. *Financial Plan:* Bootstrapping using a Shishu/Kishor MUDRA loan, offering SaaS-based subscription audits.

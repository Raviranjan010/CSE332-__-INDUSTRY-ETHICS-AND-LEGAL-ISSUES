## 🔷 SECTION A: Highest Probability MCQs (Core Syllabus & Exam Classics)

**Q1.** According to DPIIT (Startup India), what is the maximum number of years a company can be in existence to be classified as a startup?
- A) 5 years
- B) 7 years
- C) 10 years ✅
- D) 15 years

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** DPIIT defines a startup as a company that has been in existence for **less than 10 years** from its date of incorporation. This is a frequently tested factual question in LPU exams.

---

**Q2.** What is the maximum annual turnover limit for a company to be recognized as a startup under DPIIT?
- A) ₹50 crore
- B) ₹100 crore ✅
- C) ₹200 crore
- D) ₹500 crore

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** DPIIT states that a startup's annual turnover must **not exceed ₹100 crore** in any financial year since incorporation. This is a key eligibility criterion.

---

**Q3.** Which of the following is NOT a criterion for a startup to be recognized under DPIIT?
- A) Must be working towards innovation or improvement of products/services
- B) Should have high potential for employment and wealth creation
- C) Must have a minimum turnover of ₹10 crore ✅
- D) Should not be formed by splitting or reconstructing an existing business

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** DPIIT does **not** require a minimum turnover. It only sets an **upper limit** of ₹100 crore. This is a tricky question that tests whether students confuse minimum and maximum limits.

---

**Q4.** What does DPIIT stand for?
- A) Department for Promotion of Industry and International Trade
- B) Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade ✅
- C) Department for Progress of Innovation and Internal Technology
- D) Division for Promotion of IT and Investments

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** DPIIT stands for **Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade**. Note: "Internal" not "International" — a very common trick in exam MCQs.

---

**Q5.** A company can be considered a startup if it is formed by:
- A) Splitting an existing business
- B) Reconstructing an existing business
- C) A fresh incorporation with innovative products/services ✅
- D) Both A and B

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** DPIIT explicitly states that a startup must **not be formed by splitting or reconstructing** an existing business. Fresh incorporation with an innovative business model is required.

---

**Q6.** Which of the following legal structures is NOT eligible for recognition as a startup under DPIIT?
- A) Private Limited Company
- B) Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)
- C) Registered Partnership Firm
- D) Sole Proprietorship ✅

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Sole proprietorships are not eligible for DPIIT startup recognition; only Private Ltd, LLPs, or registered partnerships qualify because they offer distinct legal entity structures required for tracking equity and corporate compliances.

**Q8.** How many years of income tax exemption do eligible startups receive under the Startup India Scheme?
- A) 2 years
- B) 3 years ✅
- C) 5 years
- D) 7 years

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Under Section 80-IAC of the Income Tax Act, startups registered under DPIIT are eligible for a **3-year income tax holiday** (100% tax exemption for 3 consecutive years out of the first 10 years).

**Q9.** Under which section of the Income Tax Act do startups receive the 3-year tax exemption?
- A) Section 56(2)(viib)
- B) Section 80-IAC ✅
- C) Section 44AD
- D) Section 10(23FB)

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** **Section 80-IAC** provides the 3-year income tax holiday. Section 56(2)(viib) relates to Angel Tax. This distinction is frequently tested in tricky MCQs.

---

**Q10.** Which tax provision specifically exempts startups from paying tax on investments received from investors (Angel investors)?
- A) Section 80-IAC
- B) Section 44AB
- C) Section 56(2)(viib) ✅
- D) Section 12A

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** **Section 56(2)(viib)** of the Income Tax Act pertains to Angel Tax — the tax on excess premium received from investors. Startup India provides an **exemption** from this Angel Tax, making it easier to attract investor funding.

**Q19.** Under MUDRA, which category of loan is for new businesses with a maximum amount of ₹50,000?
- A) Tarun
- B) Kishor
- C) Shishu ✅
- D) Yuva

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** MUDRA loans are divided into 3 tiers: **Shishu** (up to ₹50,000 for new businesses), **Kishor** (₹50,000–₹5 lakh for growing businesses), and **Tarun** (₹5 lakh–₹10 lakh for established businesses).

**Q20.** Which MUDRA category covers loans from ₹50,000 to ₹5 lakh?

- B) Kishor ✅
- C) Tarun
- D) Pradhan

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** **Kishor** covers ₹50,000 to ₹5 lakh for growing businesses. Remember the order: Shishu → Kishor → Tarun (small → medium → large), which mirrors the Hindi words meaning child → adolescent → adult.

---

**Q21.** Which MUDRA category covers loans from ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh?
- A) Shishu
- B) Kishor
- C) Tarun ✅
- D) Pradhan

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** **Tarun** covers loans from ₹5 lakh up to ₹10 lakh for established micro-enterprises looking to scale up.

---

**Q22.** Arrange the MUDRA loan categories in order from smallest to largest amount:
- A) Tarun → Kishor → Shishu
- B) Kishor → Shishu → Tarun
- C) Shishu → Kishor → Tarun ✅
- D) Shishu → Tarun → Kishor

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The correct order is **Shishu (up to ₹50K) → Kishor (₹50K–₹5L) → Tarun (₹5L–₹10L)**. A helpful mnemonic: Shishu (child/new) → Kishor (adolescent/growing) → Tarun (adult/established).

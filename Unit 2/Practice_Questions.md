## 📌 PART A — SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS (2-3 Marks)

**Q1. Define Intellectual Property (IP). Why is IP protection important?**
*Answer:* Intellectual Property refers to the creations of the human mind (inventions, literary/artistic works, symbols, names, logos, designs). IP protection is important because it grants creators exclusive legal rights, rewards innovation, drives economic growth, and provides a competitive advantage.

**Q2. What is a copyright? What types of works does it protect?**
*Answer:* A copyright is an exclusive legal right given to creators to protect their original creative expressions. It protects: literary works (including computer code and databases), musical works, dramatic works, artistic works, films, and sound recordings.

**Q3. Distinguish between copyright and trademark.**
*Answer:* Copyright protects original creative and artistic expressions (books, music, software code), whereas Trademark protects brand identity (brand names, logos, symbols, slogans) that identify the source of goods or services.

**Q4. What is a "well-known mark"? Give two examples.**
*Answer:* A well-known mark is a trademark that is highly famous and recognized globally or nationally. It enjoys special protection across industries and countries even if it is not registered there. Examples: Google, Coca-Cola, McDonald's.

**Q5. Define a patent. What are the three conditions for an invention to be patented?**
*Answer:* A patent is a statutory grant by the government to an inventor, giving them exclusive rights to exclude others from making, using, or selling their invention for 20 years. The three conditions are:
1. **Novelty** (must be new, not prior art)
2. **Non-obviousness** (not obvious to a person skilled in the art)
3. **Industrial Applicability** (must have utility)

**Q6. What is the difference between a certification mark and a service mark?**
*Answer:* A certification mark indicates that goods/services meet specific quality, origin, or material standards (e.g., ISI, Agmark, Hallmark), and is *not* used by the mark owner. A service mark identifies and distinguishes the services of one provider from another (e.g., FedEx, Netflix logo).

**Q7. What is a trade secret? Give a famous example.**
*Answer:* A trade secret is confidential business information that provides a company with a competitive advantage (e.g., formula, design, compilation). Example: The secret formula for Coca-Cola.

**Q8. What is meant by "biopiracy"? Give one Indian example.**
*Answer:* Biopiracy is the unethical patenting of traditional biological knowledge (like plant properties) belonging to developing countries by foreign corporations without consent or compensation. Example: The patenting of Neem oil properties as a pesticide by a US company, which India successfully challenged.

**Q9. What is a "patent troll"? Why are they problematic?**
*Answer:* A patent troll is an entity (Non-Practicing Entity) that buys patents not to use or develop products, but solely to sue other companies for infringement to extract cash settlements. They stifle innovation by targeting vulnerable startups.

**Q10. What is "evergreening" of patents? Why is it considered unethical?**
*Answer:* Evergreening is the practice of making minor, non-therapeutic modifications to an existing patented product (like a drug) just before the patent expires, in order to get a new patent and extend the monopoly. It is unethical because it blocks generic competition and increases healthcare costs.

**Q11. What does WIPO stand for? What is its role?**
*Answer:* WIPO stands for World Intellectual Property Organization. Its role is to promote the protection of intellectual property globally through international cooperation and treaties.

**Q12. List the steps involved in filing a patent in India.**
*Answer:*
1. Invention disclosure and prior art search.
2. File provisional application (optional, establishes priority).
3. File complete specification application within 12 months.
4. Publication of application (usually 18 months from filing).
5. Request for examination (within 48 months).
6. Response to first examination report (FER).
7. Grant of patent.

**Q13. What is Freedom to Operate (FTO)?**
*Answer:* FTO is a search and legal analysis conducted before launching a product or service to verify that it does not infringe on any active patents held by others in that market.

**Q14. What is the Nice Classification system for trademarks?**
*Answer:* The Nice Classification is an international classification system that groups goods and services into 45 distinct classes (Classes 1–34 for goods, 35–45 for services) for the purpose of registering trademarks.

**Q15. Mention three major patent databases and their coverage.**
*Answer:*
1. Google Patents (global patent coverage)
2. Espacenet (European Patent Office coverage)
3. IP India Database (Indian patents)

## 📌 PART B — LONG ANSWER QUESTIONS (5-7 Marks)

**Q16. Explain the four main types of Intellectual Property (Copyright, Trademark, Patent, Trade Secret) with examples from the IT industry.**
*Answer:*
* **Copyright:** Protects the expression of ideas, including software code and databases. Example: Android's source code, or the code of Windows 11.
* **Trademark:** Protects brand names and symbols. Example: Google's colorful wordmark, Apple's bitten apple logo.
* **Patent:** Protects new, non-obvious technological processes and devices. Example: Apple's "Slide to Unlock" UI patent, or Qualcomm's 5G transmission patents.
* **Trade Secret:** Protects confidential formulas and business methods. Example: Google's Search PageRank algorithm, which is kept completely confidential.

**Q17. Describe the different kinds of marks — brand names, logos, signatures, symbols, well-known marks, certification marks, and service marks — with examples.**
*Answer:*
* **Brand Name:** Words identifying products/companies (e.g., Infosys).
* **Logo:** Graphic symbol (e.g., Nike Swoosh).
* **Signature:** Handwriting brand (e.g., Walt Disney's signature).
* **Symbol:** Graphic mark without words (e.g., Mercedes three-point star).
* **Well-known Mark:** Globally recognized mark protected even without local registration (e.g., Coca-Cola).
* **Certification Mark:** Assures quality standards (e.g., ISI, Hallmark).
* **Service Mark:** Identifies services rather than goods (e.g., Netflix service mark).

**Q18. What is a patent? Explain the complete patent registration process in India with all steps.**
*Answer:*
A patent is an exclusive right granted for a novel, non-obvious, and industrially useful invention. In India, the process:
1. **Prior Art Search:** Check databases to ensure novelty.
2. **Provisional Specification (Form 2):** Secure priority date.
3. **Complete Specification (within 12 months):** Detailed description with claims.
4. **Publication (18 months):** Public disclosure.
5. **Request for Examination:** Must request within 48 months.
6. **First Examination Report (FER):** Auditor objections.
7. **Objection Response:** Applicant complies.
8. **Patent Grant & Renewals:** Valid for 20 years, paid annually.

## 📌 PART C — CASE STUDY QUESTIONS (10 Marks)

**Q19. Case Study: Neem Biopiracy**
*Situation: A US corporation patented a method of extracting oil from Neem trees as a pesticide. Neem had been used in India for centuries for this exact purpose.*
* **What legal and ethical issues does this case raise?** It represents biopiracy — patenting traditional indigenous knowledge without consent, which violates the novelty requirement in patent law.
* **How was it resolved?** The government of India, through the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR), challenged the patent at the European Patent Office. India presented ancient Sanskrit texts proving Neem's pesticide properties were prior art, resulting in the patent being overturned in 2000.
* **What is the takeaway?** Traditional knowledge databases (like India's TKDL - Traditional Knowledge Digital Library) are essential to prevent biopiracy.

## 📌 PART D — MCQ QUESTIONS WITH SOLUTIONS

**MCQ 1. The symbol © represents:**
- a) Trademark
- b) Patent
- c) **Copyright** ✓
- d) Certification mark
*Solution: c) © is the international symbol for copyright.*

**MCQ 2. Duration of copyright for an individual creator in India is:**
- a) 50 years from creation
- b) 20 years from filing
- c) **Life of the creator + 60 years** ✓
- d) 99 years from publication
*Solution: c) In India, individual copyright lasts for the creator's lifetime plus 60 years.*

**MCQ 3. A patent provides protection for:**
- a) 10 years
- b) 15 years
- c) **20 years** ✓
- d) 25 years
*Solution: c) Patent protection duration is globally standard at 20 years from filing.*

**MCQ 4. Which of the following CANNOT be patented in India?**
- a) New drug synthesis process
- b) Machine to filter water
- c) **Mathematical algorithm** ✓
- d) Improved engine mechanism
*Solution: c) Mathematical methods and software algorithms "per se" are not patentable under Section 3 of the Indian Patents Act.*

**MCQ 5. The ® symbol means the trademark is:**
- a) Royalty-free
- b) Under application
- c) **Registered** ✓
- d) Reserved by the government
*Solution: c) ® means a trademark has been officially registered; ™ is used for unregistered marks.*

**MCQ 6. WIPO stands for:**
- a) World Intellectual Protection Organization
- b) **World Intellectual Property Organization** ✓
- c) World Internet Policy Office
- d) Worldwide IP Operations
*Solution: b) WIPO is the global UN agency for IP policy.*

**MCQ 7. The ISI mark is an example of a:**
- a) Service mark
- b) Trademark
- c) **Certification mark** ✓
- d) Well-known mark
*Solution: c) The ISI mark certifies quality and safety standards.*

**MCQ 8. Coca-Cola's secret formula is an example of:**
- a) Patent
- b) Copyright
- c) Trademark
- d) **Trade secret** ✓
*Solution: d) It is kept confidential indefinitely; patenting would force public disclosure.*

**MCQ 9. In the Novartis vs. Union of India case, the Indian Supreme Court rejected the patent because of:**
- a) Prior art issue
- b) **Section 3(d) — evergreening provision** ✓
- c) The drug was not useful
- d) Patent was already filed in the US
*Solution: b) Section 3(d) prevents patenting minor modifications to drugs without proof of increased therapeutic efficacy.*

**MCQ 10. A company that buys patents only to sue others (without using the patent) is called:**
- a) Patent holder
- b) Licensor
- c) **Patent troll** ✓
- d) Patent assignee
*Solution: c) Patent trolls exploit litigation rather than build products.*

**MCQ 11. Which law governs trademarks in India?**
- a) Indian Copyright Act, 1957
- b) Patents Act, 1970
- c) **Trade Marks Act, 1999** ✓
- d) FEMA, 1999
*Solution: c) The Trade Marks Act, 1999 governs Indian trademarks.*

**MCQ 12. "Evergreening" in pharmaceutical patents refers to:**
- a) Creating green technology patents
- b) **Making minor changes to extend patent life** ✓
- c) Patenting agricultural inventions
- d) Renewing a patent before expiry
*Solution: b) Evergreening extends monopoly status through minor improvements.*

**MCQ 13. Freedom to Operate (FTO) analysis is done:**
- a) After a lawsuit is filed
- b) **Before launching a product/service to avoid infringement** ✓
- c) After patent grant
- d) During trademark registration
*Solution: b) FTO ensures the product can be launched without infringing active patents.*

**MCQ 14. India's national IP office is managed by:**
- a) SEBI
- b) RBI
- c) **CGPDTM (Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks)** ✓
- d) MCA
*Solution: c) CGPDTM manages patents, designs, and trademarks.*

**MCQ 15. The Neem tree biopiracy case was resolved in whose favor?**
- a) US companies
- b) European Patent Office
- c) **India** ✓
- d) WIPO
*Solution: c) The patent was revoked, validating India's claim of traditional knowledge.*

**MCQ 1. Intellectual Property (IP) differs from physical property primarily because IP is:**
- A) Governed exclusively by international criminal courts.
- B) **Intangible and a product of the human intellect.** ✓
- C) Exempt from all national trade tariffs and income taxes.
- D) Permanent and cannot expire under any circumstances.
- E) Excluded from the scope of private contract agreements.
- F) Non-transferable to third-party organizations.
* **Correct Answer:** B
* **Explanation:**
  * **Why B is correct:** IP represents creations of the mind (inventions, literary works, logos) and is intangible.
  * **Why others are incorrect:** IP is civil property (A), taxable (C), time-bound (D), transferable (F), and subject to contracts (E).

**MCQ 2. Which of the following conditions is NOT required for an invention to be patentable in India?**
- A) Novelty (the invention must be new).
- B) Inventive step (it must be non-obvious to a person skilled in the art).
- C) Industrial applicability (it must be useful and capable of industrial use).
- D) **The invention must be a computer program or mathematical algorithm per se.** ✓
- E) It must not fall under the list of non-patentable subject matter in Section 3.
- F) It must be fully disclosed in a complete specification.
* **Correct Answer:** D
* **Explanation:**
  * **Why D is correct:** Under Section 3(k) of the Indian Patents Act, mathematical methods, business methods, and computer programs "per se" are not patentable.
  * **Why others are incorrect:** Novelty (A), Non-obviousness (B), Utility (C), non-excludability (E), and full disclosure (F) are mandatory statutory requirements.

## 📌 PART D: 10 SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS (2-3 Marks)

**Q86. Define Intellectual Property (IP). Why is IP protection important?**
* *Answer:* Intellectual Property refers to the creations of the human mind (inventions, literary/artistic works, symbols, names, logos, designs). IP protection is important because it grants creators exclusive legal rights, rewards innovation, drives economic growth, and provides a competitive advantage.

**Q87. What is a copyright? What types of works does it protect?**
* *Answer:* A copyright is an exclusive legal right given to creators to protect their original creative expressions. It protects: literary works (including computer code and databases), musical works, dramatic works, artistic works, films, and sound recordings.

**Q88. Distinguish between copyright and trademark.**
* *Answer:* Copyright protects original creative and artistic expressions (books, music, software code), whereas Trademark protects brand identity (brand names, logos, symbols, slogans) that identify the source of goods or services.

**Q89. What is a "well-known mark"? Give two examples.**
* *Answer:* A well-known mark is a trademark that is highly famous and recognized globally or nationally. It enjoys special protection across industries and countries even if it is not registered there. Examples: Google, Coca-Cola, McDonald's.

**Q90. Define a patent. What are the three conditions for an invention to be patented?**
* *Answer:* A patent is a statutory grant by the government to an inventor, giving them exclusive rights to exclude others from making, using, or selling their invention for 20 years. The three conditions are:
  1. **Novelty** (must be new, not prior art)
  2. **Non-obviousness** (not obvious to a person skilled in the art)
  3. **Industrial Applicability** (must have utility)

**Q91. What is the difference between a certification mark and a service mark?**
* *Answer:* A certification mark indicates that goods/services meet specific quality, origin, or material standards (e.g., ISI, Agmark, Hallmark), and is *not* used by the mark owner. A service mark identifies and distinguishes the services of one provider from another (e.g., FedEx, Netflix logo).

**Q92. What is a trade secret? Give a famous example.**
* *Answer:* A trade secret is confidential business information that provides a company with a competitive advantage (e.g., formula, design, compilation). Example: The secret formula for Coca-Cola.

**Q93. What is meant by "biopiracy"? Give one Indian example.**
* *Answer:* Biopiracy is the unethical patenting of traditional biological knowledge (like plant properties) belonging to developing countries by foreign corporations without consent or compensation. Example: The patenting of Neem oil properties as a pesticide by a US company, which India successfully challenged.

**Q94. What is a "patent troll"? Why are they problematic?**
* *Answer:* A patent troll is an entity (Non-Practicing Entity) that buys patents not to use or develop products, but solely to sue other companies for infringement to extract cash settlements. They stifle innovation by targeting vulnerable startups.

**Q95. What is "evergreening" of patents? Why is it considered unethical?**
* *Answer:* Evergreening is the practice of making minor, non-therapeutic modifications to an existing patented product (like a drug) just before the patent expires, in order to get a new patent and extend the monopoly. It is unethical because it blocks generic competition and increases healthcare costs.

## 📌 PART E: 5 LONG / CASE STUDY QUESTIONS (10 Marks)

**Q96. Case Study: Neem Biopiracy**
*Situation: A US corporation patented a method of extracting oil from Neem trees as a pesticide. Neem had been used in India for centuries for this exact purpose.*
* **What legal and ethical issues does this case raise?** It represents biopiracy — patenting traditional indigenous knowledge without consent, which violates the novelty requirement in patent law.
* **How was it resolved?** The government of India, through the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR), challenged the patent at the European Patent Office. India presented ancient Sanskrit texts proving Neem's pesticide properties were prior art, resulting in the patent being overturned in 2000.
* **What is the takeaway?** Traditional knowledge databases (like India's TKDL - Traditional Knowledge Digital Library) are essential to prevent biopiracy.

**Q97. Explain the Novartis vs. Union of India (2013) Case and its impact on the Indian Pharmaceutical Industry.**
* *Answer:*
  * **Context:** Swiss company Novartis sought to patent the beta crystalline form of imatinib mesylate (Gleevec), a cancer drug. India's patent controller rejected the claim because the basic molecule was already known, citing Section 3(d) of the Patents Act.
  * **Supreme Court Ruling:** The Supreme Court upheld the rejection. It ruled that the new form did not show enhanced therapeutic efficacy over the known substance, thus failing the test of Section 3(d).
  * **Impact:** 
    1. It prevented drug companies from "evergreening" old drugs to maintain high prices.
    2. It protected India's generic pharmaceutical industry, ensuring cheap access to life-saving medicines.
    3. It struck a balance between rewarding true innovation and protecting public health.

**Q98. Differentiate between Trademark Infringement and Passing Off. Explain the statutory provisions.**
* *Answer:*
  * **Registration Requirement:** Infringement is a statutory remedy for registered trademarks. Passing off is a common law remedy protecting unregistered trademarks.
  * **Statutory Basis:** Infringement is governed by Section 29 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999. Passing off is recognized under Section 27(2) but governed by common law tort rules.
  * **Burden of Proof:** In infringement, proving the registration certificate and deceptive similarity is sufficient. In passing off, the plaintiff must prove the "Classical Trinity" (Goodwill, Misrepresentation, Damage).
  * **Jurisdiction:** Infringement suits are filed under the Trade Marks Act, making it easier to secure national injunctions. Passing off requires localized proof of trade reputation.

**Q99. Detail the steps involved in registering a patent in India under the Patents Act, 1970.**
* *Answer:*
  1. **Invention Disclosure:** Document the details of the invention.
  2. **Prior Art Search:** Search Google Patents, Espacenet, and IP India to ensure novelty.
  3. **Filing Application:** File provisional specification (Form 2) to secure a priority date.
  4. **Complete Specification:** File full details and claims within 12 months.
  5. **Publication:** The patent office publishes the application after 18 months.
  6. **Request for Examination:** Must request examination within 48 months.
  7. **First Examination Report (FER):** Examiner raises objections or issues.
  8. **Response to FER:** Applicant responds to clear all objections.
  9. **Patent Grant:** If satisfied, the Controller grants the patent for 20 years.

**Q100. Analyze the differences between Patents, Copyrights, Trademarks, and Trade Secrets, detailing their terms and registration requirements.**
* *Answer:*
  * **Patents:** Protect functional technological inventions. Term is 20 years from filing. Mandatory registration required. (e.g. new engine system).
  * **Copyrights:** Protect original creative expressions (books, music, software code). Term is life + 60 years (India). Registration is automatic upon creation, though recommended. (e.g. software code).
  * **Trademarks:** Protect brand identity, logos, and signs. Term is 10 years, renewable indefinitely. Registration is recommended for statutory infringement protection. (e.g. corporate logo).
  * **Trade Secrets:** Protect confidential business information. Term is indefinite as long as kept secret. No registration required (protected by NDAs and security measures). (e.g. secret drink formula).

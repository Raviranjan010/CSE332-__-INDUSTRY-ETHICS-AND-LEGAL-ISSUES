## 📌 PART A — SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS (2-3 Marks)

**Q1. What is an executive summary? Why is it written last but placed first?**
*Answer:* An executive summary is a brief 1-2 page overview of a business plan, highlighting the problem, solution, target market, financial projection, and funding requirements. It is written last because it summarizes the completed business plan details, but placed first because it is the first thing investors read to determine if they want to read the rest of the plan.

**Q2. Differentiate between mission statement and vision statement. Give examples.**
*Answer:* A mission statement defines the company's current business, its objectives, and how it reaches those objectives (what the company does today). Example: "To organize the world's information and make it universally accessible and useful." (Google). A vision statement describes where the company aspires to be in the future (what the company wants to become tomorrow). Example: "To provide access to the world's information in one click."

**Q3. What is an MVP (Minimum Viable Product)? Why is it important?**
*Answer:* An MVP is the simplest version of a product containing only the core features required to deploy it to early adopters and gather feedback. It is important because it allows startups to validate assumptions, test product-market fit, and iterate with minimal capital and time investment.

**Q4. Explain the TAM-SAM-SOM framework with an example.**
*Answer:* It is a framework to size the target market:
1. **TAM (Total Addressable Market):** The total global market demand for a product/service. Example: Global food delivery market.
2. **SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market):** The portion of TAM targeted by your product within geographic/logistics reach. Example: Indian food delivery market.
3. **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market):** The portion of SAM that your company can realistically capture in the short term. Example: Food delivery market in Pune for a new regional startup.

**Q5. What is a Cap Table? Why is it important for a startup?**
*Answer:* A Capitalization Table (Cap Table) is a spreadsheet showing the ownership equity of a company, including stock shares, options, and ownership percentages of founders, employees, and investors. It is important because it tracks dilution and shows who owns what percentage of the company during funding rounds.

**Q6. Define burn rate and runway. How are they calculated?**
*Answer:*
* **Burn Rate:** The monthly rate at which a startup spends its cash reserves (Net Burn = Total Monthly Cash Spent - Monthly Revenue).
* **Runway:** The number of months a startup can operate before running out of cash.
* **Runway Formula:** Cash Balance ÷ Net Burn Rate.

**Q7. What is Product-Led Growth (PLG)? Give a real example.**
*Answer:* PLG is a business methodology where user acquisition, retention, and expansion are driven primarily by the product itself (through freemium models, virality, self-serve onboarding). Example: Zoom, Slack, or Canva.

**Q8. Explain the Agile/Scrum methodology for IT startup development.**
*Answer:* Agile/Scrum is an iterative software development framework. Development is broken into short, fixed-length cycles called "Sprints" (usually 2 weeks). Features are planned, coded, and tested in each sprint, allowing startups to incorporate user feedback quickly and pivot if needed.

**Q9. What is customer churn? How does it impact an IT startup?**
*Answer:* Customer churn is the percentage of subscribers who cancel their subscription over a given period. It impacts IT startups because high churn reduces Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR), increases Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) pressure, and signals lack of product-market fit.

**Q10. Differentiate between ARR and MRR.**
*Answer:*
* **MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue):** The predictable recurring revenue a SaaS startup expects to earn each month.
* **ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue):** The annualized version of MRR (MRR x 12), representing predictable recurring revenue over a year.

**Q11. What is a break-even analysis? Give a formula with example.**
*Answer:* Break-even analysis calculates the point at which total revenue equals total cost (resulting in zero profit).
* **Formula:** Break-Even Point (Units) = Fixed Costs ÷ (Selling Price per unit - Variable Cost per unit).
* *Example:* Fixed costs = ₹1,00,000, Price = ₹100, Variable Cost = ₹50. Break-Even = 1,00,000 ÷ (100 - 50) = 2,000 units.

**Q12. List five key roles in an IT startup and their responsibilities.**
*Answer:*
1. CEO (Chief Executive Officer): Overall vision and fundraising.
2. CTO (Chief Technology Officer): Technology architecture and product delivery.
3. Product Manager: Translates customer needs into product features.
4. Lead Engineer/Developer: Writing code and building the software.
5. Sales/Marketing Manager: Customer acquisition and growth.

**Q13. What is an ESOP? How does it help startups attract talent?**
*Answer:* Employee Stock Option Plan (ESOP) grants employees the option to buy company shares at a discounted price in the future. It helps startups attract talent by offering them equity ownership, aligning employee interest with company success, and compensating for lower initial cash salaries.

**Q14. What should be included in the appendices of a business plan?**
*Answer:* Legal documents, founder resumes, product mockup images, patents/trademark filings, detailed financial tables, and letter of intent from initial pilot customers.

**Q15. Why is refining the business plan important? When should it be done?**
*Answer:* Refining the plan is important because initial assumptions about customers, pricing, and competitors are often incorrect. It should be done regularly when new user feedback is received, when market dynamics change, or before pitch sessions to secure new funding rounds.

## 📌 PART B — LONG ANSWER QUESTIONS (5-7 Marks)

**Q16. Explain the complete structure of an IT startup business plan. What are the key components and why is each important?**
*Answer:*
A complete IT business plan contains:
1. **Executive Summary:** Concise business overview.
2. **Company Description:** Legal structure, history, and mission/vision.
3. **Products & Services:** Detailed description of the software/hardware and MVP results.
4. **Marketing Plan:** Target market, competitor analysis, pricing model (SaaS, freemium), and customer acquisition strategy.
5. **Operational Plan:** Dev workflow (Agile), customer support, hosting infrastructure (AWS/Azure).
6. **Management & Org:** Cap table, roles (CEO, CTO, Lead Dev).
7. **Financial Plan:** Expenses, burn rate, break-even analysis, and 3-year cash flow projections.
8. **Appendices:** Supporting legal and technical documents.

**Q17. What is financial planning for an IT startup? Explain key financial statements: P&L, Cash Flow, and Balance Sheet in the context of a startup.**
*Answer:*
* **Financial Planning:** Predicting expenses, revenues, and funding needs to ensure the startup doesn't run out of cash.
* **P&L (Profit & Loss) Statement:** Shows revenues, cost of goods sold, operating expenses, and net profit over a period. Helps understand if the business is operationally profitable.
* **Cash Flow Statement:** Tracks the physical inflow and outflow of cash. Crucial for startups because cash determines survival (runway), even if the P&L shows book profits.
* **Balance Sheet:** A snapshot of assets (cash, equipment), liabilities (loans), and equity (founder/investor shares) at a specific point in time.

## 📌 PART C — CASE STUDY QUESTIONS (10 Marks)

**Q18. Case Study: Airbnb MVP**
*Situation: The founders of Airbnb could not pay rent. They noticed a design conference was coming to town, and all hotels were booked. They bought air mattresses, set up a simple blog site offering lodging and breakfast for $80/night, and secured three guests.*
* **Was this a successful MVP? Why?** Yes. It validated the core assumption — that strangers were willing to pay to sleep in someone's home, and that hosts were willing to let them. It required minimal capital (blog site + air mattresses) and led directly to the scaling of Airbnb.
* **How does this apply to IT startups?** Startups must build the simplest possible version to test customer demand before investing time and money in writing millions of lines of code.
